# Design judgment

Use this reference when a request requires a visual direction, aesthetic diagnosis, comparative exploration, or selection between plausible interfaces.

## Start with intent

Write a compact thesis before styling:

- the primary user task and what deserves first attention;
- the time, attention, trust, information, or permission the interface requests;
- the emotion the experience should reinforce;
- the contexts and abilities it must accommodate;
- familiar product and platform conventions it should preserve;
- the one or two traits that should make it recognizable.

Do not translate “Apple-like,” “premium,” “minimal,” or another style label directly into surface treatments. Resolve the underlying request: purpose, restraint, hierarchy, platform fit, tactility, confidence, warmth, precision, or another testable quality.

## Define a visual grammar

Describe roles and relationships rather than page-specific decoration:

- hierarchy and intended scan order;
- type roles, measure, leading, and density;
- spacing, grouping, alignment, and rhythm;
- color roles and contrast behavior;
- material, border, depth, shape, and icon rules;
- imagery or data-visualization behavior;
- motion's communicative job;
- distinctive traits and where restraint protects them.

A coherent grammar must pass two questions: **Does it belong to the system?** and **Could it belong specifically to this product?** Failing the first produces inconsistency; failing the second produces generic sameness.

For multi-platform products, keep object meaning, action consequence, status semantics, and token roles shared. Let platform-native layout, components, iconography, input behavior, density, and motion differ when that improves familiarity or context fit. Consistency means predictable meaning, not identical pixels.

## Diverge when comparison changes the decision

Create two or three named directions when the work is brand-defining, spans several surfaces, introduces a new interaction language, is costly to reverse, or has credible unresolved alternatives. Hold approved structure and content constant. Declare the dimensions that change, such as density, typographic voice, information emphasis, material treatment, imagery, or motion character.

Color swaps, corner-radius changes, and arbitrary theme variants are not meaningful divergence. If a direction changes the hub object, page architecture, or primary flow, return it to Charlie as structural work.

For an established system or reversible extension, reuse the system and record why visual divergence would add cost without useful evidence.

## Diagnose before prescribing

| Lens | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Does the dominant element advance the primary task? What does not earn its place? |
| Hierarchy | Where does the eye go first, second, and third? Are emphasis and de-emphasis intentional? |
| Grouping | Do proximity, rhythm, alignment, and whitespace express the right relationships? |
| Legibility | Does the design survive real data, localization, zoom, contrast preferences, and large text? |
| Coherence | Do repeated roles behave and look consistently across surfaces and states? |
| Character | Does expression reinforce the intended emotion and product identity without taxing comprehension? |
| Interaction | Are controls familiar, feedback immediate, motion purposeful, and recovery clear? |
| Resilience | Does the core outcome survive relevant device, input, capability, and failure conditions? |

Tie findings to visible evidence, a principle, or an explicit assumption. “Feels cleaner,” “more modern,” and “more premium” are not diagnoses until the relevant hierarchy, clutter, rhythm, material, behavior, or emotional-fit change is named.

## Select without false precision

First eliminate directions that fail semantics, task accessibility, recovery, critical-state coverage, or responsive integrity. Do not average a failed gate into a passing score.

Among valid directions, compare purpose fit, evidence, system coherence, product character, platform fit, effort, reversibility, and known risk. Record the winner, credible runner-up, why it lost, useful elements remixed from it, accepted tradeoffs, and remaining evidence debt.

Use scoring only as a conversation aid. A single weighted “beauty” number disguises judgment rather than improving it. Consequential brand, strategy, and scope choices return to the human.

## Source basis

- [Apple, Principles of great design](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/250/): purpose, agency, familiarity, flexibility, simplicity, craft, and delight.
- [Apple, Design foundations from idea to interface](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/359/): diagnosis and progressive refinement across structure, navigation, content, and visual design.
- [Airbnb design principles](https://principles.design/examples/airbnb-design-principles): unified, universal, iconic, and conversational qualities.
- [Braun, 100 years of design](https://www.braunhousehold.com/en-us/e/braun-100-years): usefulness, intelligibility, honesty, detail, longevity, and reduction.
- [Refactoring UI](https://refactoringui.com/): concrete hierarchy, spacing, and de-emphasis tactics.
