# Challenge: pdf-case-studies portfolio set

## What I am challenging

Three commitments implied by drafting all three retail stories as peer IDEAS sections next to Classroom, Green Loom, Design Dash, and Many Hats:

1. That older Amazon / Home Depot retail stories strengthen the current portfolio signal.
2. That Home Depot price adjustments deserves a standalone case study with only directional impact.
3. That the A-to-z narrative can use first-person design ownership without over-claiming a team email change and first-claim coverage program as sole-designer impact.

Drafts may proceed for interview prep. This challenge does not withdraw if they do.

## Strongest case against

The live portfolio already shows recent, differentiated work: Classroom (education product systems), Green Loom, Design Dash (method), and Many Hats (agent practice). Adding three employer-sensitive retail stories from an image deck with unverified metrics increases confidentiality risk and narrative sameness (“I researched, I designed, metrics improved”) faster than it increases signal. Price adjustments is process-rich and outcome-poor. A-to-z’s strongest slide is an email plus a coverage policy—easy to misread as one designer’s unilateral win. Publishing any of them without human gates converts interview scaffolding into public claims the career record still marks unverified.

What is strong and must remain if the direction is revised.

- Metric traceability and missing-figure markers in the drafts.
- Separation of Aaron’s email/tenets work from first-claim coverage and shipping on A-to-z.
- Explicit directional-only language on price adjustments.
- Omission of `**%` and of the placeholder journey as a finished artifact.
- Cipher’s default: prose and placeholders, no PDF crops.

## Verdict

**Proceed with conditions.**

Conditions:

1. Treat sections 9–11 as interview drafts, not publish commitments.
2. Do not build public pages, homepage cards, or résumé updates until human gates 1–4 answer; default answer while silent is **no**.
3. Do not let price adjustments become a public page without either quantified impact or an explicit human choice to publish a process-only story.
4. Keep A-to-z attribution split; if a sentence assigns call-volume or coverage-investment outcomes to “I” alone, revise or cut.
5. Preserve this challenge file’s dissent even if drafts remain.

Fallback aligned with direction option D: if the set must shrink, keep A-to-z for interviews, prefer curbside over price adjustments for any single Home Depot public slot, and drop or nest price adjustments.
