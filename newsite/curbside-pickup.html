The Home Depot / Curbside Pickup

The wait was short.
The uncertainty was not.

Customers parked and waited without knowing whether the store knew they were there. Some went inside and changed their pickup plan.

I used field findings to win support for email-based arrival timing and app check-in feedback in a limited pilot.

My contribution
Staff UX Designer; field research, pilot proposal, pickup email, and app notifications
Partners
Customers, associates, store managers, product partners, and pickup stakeholders
Pilot
Early-2020 context; 50 stores over 60 days

An uncertain customer
became extra work in the store.

During store visits, I repeatedly saw customers park, wait, then walk to the service desk to switch to in-store pickup. A fulfillment change could send associates moving an order from one end of the store to the other.

Interviews with customers, associates, and managers exposed the feedback gap. Customers could call or open the app and still not know whether check-in had worked or an order was on its way.

The arrival signal required the app, login, and a Wi-Fi connection, yet gave customers no acknowledgment. Weak parking-lot signals and nearby errands made detection unreliable as a guide to when customers needed help. Email offered another way to share arrival timing.

Broaden pickup access,
even beyond the app.

The running strategy emphasized marketing the app and encouraging login. More app use could help operations, but would not close the feedback gap on its own.

I proposed ETA choices and check-in through pickup emails, paired with app notifications for success, failure recovery, and order progress. Making email pickup easier could reduce the incentive to download the app.

I made that tradeoff explicit in a proposal deck, using field findings and the operational disruption caused by fulfillment changes. Product partners and pickup stakeholders supported a limited regional pilot before a broader commitment.

Help the store anticipate arrival and tell customers whether their check-in worked.

One selected pickup method.
A clear next step in every state.

The old confirmation email promoted other fulfillment options, making the selected method unclear. I focused the message on curbside instructions, ETA choices, help, and Spanish-language access.

App messaging covered three states: arrival acknowledged with a ten-minute expectation, check-in failed with instructions to call the store, and order coming out. The recovery path remained explicit when systems or connectivity failed.

What changed

Clearer expectations
in a 50-store pilot.

The project presentation reports these findings from a 60-day pilot in 50 stores: approximately 90% of customers said their order arrived when expected, and fewer than 10% called to change pickup method, compared with a baseline around 30%.

Reported average door-to-door time was 3.8 minutes with an ETA and 4.5 minutes without. The pilot combined communication and operational changes.

What I took forward

Channel adoption and service success need to be considered together. Arrival information helps associates prepare; acknowledgement helps customers know what to do while they wait.
