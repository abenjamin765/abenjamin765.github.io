# Case-study evaluation rubric

October 25, 2026 · Editorial standard for the ten cases linked from Work.

## Purpose and evidence limits

Evaluate how well each case lets a Senior/Lead/Staff/Principal UX or product-design reviewer understand the problem, Aaron's contribution, the decisions, the craft, and the consequence. These are editorial assessments, not hiring predictions or independent verification of career claims.

The [research library](README.md) separates three kinds of sources. The Wall of Portfolios notes inspected homepages, not case interiors. The winning-portfolios report interpreted a curated twelve-site sample including interiors. The design-system report supplies normative interface/accessibility guidance. Do not combine their samples, treat a reported pattern as a hiring-manager study, or treat a recommended pattern as a proven cause of hiring success. Preserve each note's Observed/Inferred/Borrowed/Prescribed distinctions when explaining scores.

Our weights, score anchors, readiness threshold, and case-specific adaptations below are **editorial decisions**, approved for this project. They are not measured findings from the research.

## Scoring

Score each criterion from 0 to 4. Use the criterion's Strong column to calibrate the middle-upper anchor; cite actual passages, artifacts, or browser checks rather than impression alone.

| Score | Anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 — Absent | The case provides no assessable support for this criterion. |
| 1 — Weak | It names the topic or makes a claim but omits the essential supporting context/evidence. |
| 2 — Adequate | The reader can assess the main point, but a material part remains vague, disconnected, or hard to use. |
| 3 — Strong | Meets the criterion's concrete requirements with specific, proportionate support and honest limits. |
| 4 — Exceptional | Goes beyond Strong with unusually inspectable reasoning, alternatives, artifacts, or consequence; the extra detail earns its space. Length and impressive numbers alone do not qualify. |

**Total = Σ(weight × score ÷ 4).** Report the total out of 100 without rounding up to satisfy the threshold. A 3 on every criterion equals 75, not 80.

**Editorial readiness target:** at least 80/100; no criterion below 2; Ownership, Judgment, and Consequences each at least 3. Below-target cases remain below target until new evidence or a better implementation warrants a higher score. Record baseline and final scores separately; do not carry forward scores from the older rubric.

**Blocking conditions override totals:** unsupported material claims, misleading artifact provenance, broken essential navigation, or an inaccessible essential interaction. Record the blocker and remediation; passing an automated accessibility check does not prove full accessibility.

## Apply fairly across case types

- Professional product cases: distinguish personal design contribution from team delivery; distinguish rollout/pilot scope from business causality. Qualitative findings are legitimate evidence. Do not manufacture percentages or reveal confidential figures.
- Leadership cases: show the mechanism of influence, contributed artifacts/training, collaborators, constraints, and documented program changes. Do not imply direct reports or a formal mandate from mentoring activity.
- Independent projects: identify current maturity and fictional examples. Evaluate decisions, inspectable artifacts, existing validation, honest limitations, and a proposed learning approach. Shipped revenue/adoption is not required, but intended benefit is not observed impact.
- Confidential work: explain shareable decisions and scope; do not invent replacement screens or a numeric proxy for unavailable evidence.
- Do not pad cases to a prescribed reading time or force the same chapter count. Keep essential decisions/results visible; reserve disclosures for supporting depth.

## Evaluation record and evidence-gap policy

For each case record: source snapshot/date; seven baseline scores and total; specific evidence and gaps; planned edits; seven final scores and total; browser verification; unresolved questions; and readiness/blocker status. Baseline source-level craft scores identify available artifacts, not proof of their final readability. Final reading-experience scores require rendered checks.

Aaron selected **pause for input** when a material fact is missing. Pause that case's unsupported revision and ask a precise question naming the missing fact and why it matters; continue other cases. Lack of a commercial metric alone does not trigger a pause when honest qualitative results or project status support the story. Never turn an evidence gap into a convenient assumption or silently hide an incomplete case from Work.
