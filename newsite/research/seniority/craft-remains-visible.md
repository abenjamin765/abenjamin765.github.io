# Craft stays visible when the altitude rises

**Claim:** The report says senior, lead, staff, and principal work still shows product or interface detail. The detail proves a different point.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (report’s account):** Niklas combines direction and rollout with information architecture, interaction, motion, and reusable patterns. Rishabh’s leadership case still includes home states, eKYC flows, components, and localization. Kat’s principal portfolio still uses feature-level case studies.

**Inferred:** “Senior” does not mean replacing product evidence with management philosophy. Lead portfolios should include enough interaction or system craft that reviewers can see the standard being led toward. The comparison table says craft is high at entry, mid, and senior; must remain visible at lead; representative depth at staff; selectively deep at principal.

## Implication for this portfolio

A staff-scoped work story can be shorter and still show one concrete surface. Dropping the product evidence in favor of a philosophy paragraph is the move the report argues against.

## Limit

Three examples. The table is a synthesis, not a count of screens per level.
