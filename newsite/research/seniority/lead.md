# Lead: product judgment and team effect

**Claim:** The report says a lead portfolio has two audiences: can this person still make product decisions, and will the team get better.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Inferred targets:** 3 product or strategy stories plus 1 leadership story. Depth variable. Central question: do they make a team more effective while preserving product quality?

**Inferred template:** Business goal, team topology, design strategy, allocation, operating mechanism, a pivotal craft decision, stakeholder conflict, launch, product outcome and team outcome, lesson.

**Observed example:** Rishabh shows five individual outcomes and an operating manual with principles the report names as outcome-first thinking, systems over heroics, and prototyping to settle debates. DayUp describes a three-designer team dividing journeys, critiquing together, reusing patterns, managing client decisions, and handling a critical path. The product had not launched; he lists intended measures instead of fake results.

**Inferred metrics:** Product evidence and organizational evidence together: conversion, revenue, or quality, alongside delivery time, reuse, defect reduction, design-system adoption, designer growth, or stakeholder decision velocity. The report says not to make the lead portfolio purely managerial.

## Disagreement

The homepage notes treat Rishabh’s homepage as design-manager positioning, useful as contrast for an IC case portfolio. The report treats the same site as a model for a lead portfolio. Both are in the sources. For this site’s homepage, the notes’ IC instruction wins unless the human decides the portfolio is a management case. See `../what-not-to-copy/leadership-homepage-for-an-ic-site.md`.
