# Entry: show reasoning, not invented revenue

**Claim:** The report says an entry portfolio should make learning, foundational reasoning, and craft inspectable, and should not invent senior revenue attribution.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Inferred targets:** 2–3 hero cases, about 5–8 minutes each. Central question: can this person reason through an unfamiliar problem? Evidence that matters most: research quality, iteration, testing, role clarity, learning.

**Inferred template:** Snapshot, problem, what you needed to learn, research, pivotal insight, alternatives, prototype, test, final, result, what you would change.

**Observed example:** Michelle Chen’s Pic2Split case, as described: a six-person usability study produces a design requirement around AI trust, and a later test leads the team to replace a multi-screen flow with a one-page version. Completion time in the metric table is 3:10 to 1:45. Positioning line quoted: “I ship the front-end.”

**Inferred metrics:** Task success, completion time, SEQ or SUS-type measures, errors, comprehension, prototype conversion, accessibility issues resolved, side-project adoption, or test results. Revenue is not required.

## Implication for this portfolio

Use this file only if a story is early-career or instructional. It is the wrong interior template for the staff question in the homepage notes.

## Limit

Minute counts and the case template are synthesized. Michelle is one early-career example in a purposive sample.
