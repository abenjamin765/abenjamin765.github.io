# Principal: show a change in how the problem is framed

**Claim:** The report says principal portfolios make visible a change, over time, in how an organization understands and attacks a problem.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Inferred targets:** 3–5 strategic narratives, variable and long-horizon. Central question: can they change how the organization frames and solves important problems? Evidence: multi-year influence, executive alignment, standards, portfolio strategy, partial wins and failures.

**Observed examples:** Kat Angeles’ IA work, as described: a structural problem whose symptoms had been addressed piecemeal, a long arc, stakeholder sign-off and partial organizational progress treated as outcomes. Her site includes a nine-year overview and testimony from product, UX, and engineering colleagues. Manolo Beviá is a second archetype in the report: hybrid execution. The Cornell case spans hundreds of content items, multi-method IA work, more than 30 tree-test participants, dozens of visual explorations, development, and accessibility. Positioning lines quoted: “From owning the design system to the strategy” (Kat) and “clarity beats cleverness” (Manolo).

**Inferred metrics:** Business and user outcomes, and also adoption, organization-wide standards, strategic approvals, platform consolidation, investment unlocked, reduced systemic cost, or durable organizational capability. The report says qualitative influence is legitimate when documented precisely.

## Implication for this portfolio

Principal is in the library because the report has it. The homepage notes do not use Kat or Manolo, and they do not set this site’s altitude to principal. Rashmi is a principal motion designer in both sources and is a craft contrast, not this chapter.

## Limit

Two archetypes, both purposive. Testimonials are described, not quoted at length, except through the report’s paraphrase of what they cover.
