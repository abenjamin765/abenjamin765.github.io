# Five layers inside a strong case

**Claim:** The report says the strongest case studies repeatedly compress five kinds of information: orientation, stakes, judgment, material proof, and consequence.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`). Not observed by the homepage notes.

## Evidence

**Inferred table (report):**

| Layer | What the report says winners show |
| --- | --- |
| Orientation | Role, team, time, product or platform, sometimes scale |
| Stakes | A concrete user or business problem |
| Judgment | Decisions, options, trade-offs, constraints |
| Material proof | Research evidence, states, prototypes, system structures, testing |
| Consequence | Shipped state, metric, learning, failure, or the next measurement plan |

**Observed illustration:** Dharmesh’s Zomato case, as the report describes it: role, team, and a one-month timeline precede the problem; the case shows an ambitious direction, a card-design problem, the decision to scope down to ship, and behavioral or business impact.

## Implication for this portfolio

Use this as a checklist when editing a story, not as homepage chrome. The work row only has to carry a slice (where, what, what changed). The five layers live behind the row.

## Limit

“Repeatedly” is the report’s reading of cases it chose to review. It is not a claim that every page in the 12 used all five layers.
