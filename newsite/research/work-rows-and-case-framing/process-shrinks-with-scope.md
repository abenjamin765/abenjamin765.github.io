# Process evidence shrinks as scope grows

**Claim:** The report says portfolios look less like a UX-process textbook as seniority increases. Early cases show research and testing; staff and principal cases spend more attention on direction, systems, adoption, and influence.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (report’s contrast):** Michelle Chen’s Pic2Split and Joan Li’s ViewSonic work show research, flows, iterations, and usability testing explicitly. Niklas Peterson and Kat Angeles compress conventional artifact chronology and spend more attention on direction, systems, adoption, stakeholder alignment, standards, and long-lived influence.

**Observed (Niklas, LottieFiles, as described):** The case establishes responsibility, partners, direction, cross-site patterns, reviews, QA, rollout, reusable foundations, and +316% search-volume / +35% file-view impact. The report says it does not need to prove that he knows what a wireframe is.

**Inferred:** The level changes what the details prove, not whether details matter. Craft stays visible. See `../seniority/craft-remains-visible.md`.

## Disagreement

The homepage notes leave this as an open question and name Dharmesh, Niklas, or Vaibhav as the interiors to read next. The report believes it has answered a version of that question for a broader sample. It did not compare those interiors to Aaron’s stories.

## Implication for this portfolio

For a staff-scoped story, do not lead with a full process diagram. Show the decision, the system or craft that mattered, and what changed. Entry-style artifact stacks are the wrong template if the notes’ staff question is the one being answered.

## Limit

“Less like a textbook” is a comparison inside a purposive sample, not a rule that senior portfolios omit research.
