# A visible signal is not a WCAG audit

**Claim:** The report did not audit assistive technology, viewports, or WCAG conformance for the sample.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed:** The author says they did not conduct an exhaustive assistive-technology, viewport, or WCAG audit. The 25% figure (3/12) measures visible signaling, not compliance. Responsiveness was “evidence available in pages and projects,” not an exhaustive device or browser audit. Dynamic pages complicate literal counts.

**Observed limit:** A formal assessment would require viewport testing, keyboard traversal, accessibility-tree inspection, screen-reader testing, contrast checks, and other WCAG procedures. The report says W3C’s standards are a stronger implementation reference than the observed sample frequency.

## Implication for this portfolio

Do not cite “3/12” as a benchmark to beat with a badge. If this site claims care for accessibility, the report’s point is that the claim needs observable behavior, and that the sample mostly does not show it.

## Limit

The homepage notes are silent on keyboard, zoom, and contrast. There is no accessibility finding from that pass.
