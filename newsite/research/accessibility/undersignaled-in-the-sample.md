# Accessibility is rarely signaled in the sample

**Claim:** The report coded an explicit or readily observable accessibility signal in copy or markup for 3 of 12 sites, and calls that an opportunity only as an inference.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`). Not in the homepage notes.

## Evidence

**Observed (report):** 3/12. The three it describes: Michelle Chen explicitly describes semantic HTML, CSS, ARIA, focus states, keyboard flows, and screen-reader behavior, and cites work on a WCAG 2.1 AA government platform. Aditya’s positioning includes “Accessible, clear & intentional,” which the homepage notes also record. Rishabh’s page exposes a “Skip to content” link, and his DayUp system calls for accessible contrast and predictable states.

**Inferred (report):** Accessibility is surprisingly under-signaled. The report calls this an opportunity for differentiation.

## Implication for this portfolio

The homepage notes already record Aditya’s “accessible, clear & intentional” as a claim about the work, not as a badge row. Michelle’s pattern is to tie the claim to implementation and to a named standard. Copying the word “accessible” into the intro without that kind of evidence is the adjective problem in `../proof-and-metrics/specificity-over-adjectives.md`.

## Limit

3/12 is visible signaling in a purposive sample. It is not the share of UX portfolios that are accessible, and it is not evidence that signaling accessibility causes a hire.
