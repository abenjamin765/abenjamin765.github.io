# The second sentence is one claim, not a trait list

**Claim:** After the name, the sampled product homepages use one claim about the kind of work, not a list of adjectives or tools.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`); winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (homepage notes, E2):** Confidence high for that sample. Recorded claims include Aditya’s “I design what people feel, not what they notice. Accessible, clear & intentional.” Vaibhav’s “A designer who moves numbers.” Niklas’s “Bringing digital products to life with pixels and code.” Rishabh’s “I grow design teams that move revenue.”

**Observed exception (homepage notes):** Rashmi Saini’s page adds a moving list of skills (Rive, Lottie, micro-interactions). The notes call this the sample’s skill-cloud hero, and say the craft is motion, not product casework.

**Observed (report):** Propositions it highlights include “moves numbers” (Vaibhav), design-plus-code (Niklas), “I ship the front-end” (Michelle Chen), “systems over heroics” and revenue or team leverage (Rishabh), and “clarity beats cleverness” (Manolo Beviá). The report says these are concise professional propositions, not elaborate logos.

**Inferred (homepage notes):** A rotating list of adjectives does not appear in that sample and would compete with the portrait on this site.

## Implication for this portfolio

One claim in the intro. Do not add a rotating adjective list or a tool cloud to the hero. Rashmi’s marquee is recorded as a motion-reel pattern.

## Limit

“Not a list of adjectives” is the homepage notes’ summary of its sample. The report does not publish a count of adjective-list heroes. Michelle’s and Manolo’s lines are report-only; they were not in the nine-homepage pass.
