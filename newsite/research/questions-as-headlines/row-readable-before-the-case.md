# A work row should be readable before the case opens

**Claim:** The homepage notes say a strong row can be read without opening the case: where it happened, what it was, and what became easier, faster, or more honest.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed:** The clearest repeated unit in those notes is Dharmesh’s. Each row is company and year, small; a short project name; one sentence that says what changed for a person.

**Recorded rows:** “Meals under your budget / Built a way for budget-conscious users to browse meals that are guaranteed to land within a set price.” “Easier kitchen supply / Redesigned browsing and bulk pricing to help restaurants stock up, boosting order value.”

**Observed:** Niklas uses product name plus a sentence about what the work became, without the year. Vaibhav adds a metric under the sentence. Aditya makes the title itself the problem. Yinyee’s four works each have a practice label and a scope line, including “Monitoring experience for a new container runtime, for 40+ early-access Marketplace developers.”

**Inferred (homepage notes):** The existing case hooks already fit this job: “Eight finished. Which two needed help?” and “The wait was short. The uncertainty was not.” Those strings are the notes’ description of existing hooks. They do not appear in `newsite/index.html`, where the work panel is the placeholder “Hello.”

## Implication for this portfolio

Work rows: company and year, a title that names the situation, one sentence, and one outcome. The notes’ examples of outcomes to put on the row are “Shipped in six weeks” and “50-store pilot,” attached the way Vaibhav puts a number on the card, without making the number the title.

## Limit

Those two outcome phrases are the notes’ recommendation drawn from existing stories. This library did not re-open the stories to confirm the figures. Numbers are not required for the row to work. Dharmesh’s featured portfolio is praised for concise storytelling and does not lead with percentages.
