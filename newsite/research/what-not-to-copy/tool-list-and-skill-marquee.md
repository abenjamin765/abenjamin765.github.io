# Tool lists and skill marquees are the wrong hero

**Claim:** Tools in the hero are absent from the product-design pattern in the homepage notes. A skill marquee appears on the motion feature, and the notes call it the opposite pattern.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`); tool role in the winning-portfolios report.

## Evidence

**Observed (homepage notes):** Rashmi Saini’s moving skill list (Rive, Lottie, micro-interactions) is the sample’s skill-cloud hero. The craft is motion, not product casework. The contradiction section says this marquee works for a motion reel and is the opposite of the product-design pattern, where tools are absent from the hero.

**Inferred (homepage notes):** Skip a tool list, a skill marquee, and a process diagram in the hero.

**Observed (report):** Joan names Figma, Maze, PiwikPro, and Relume because they relate to how a research and design proposition was validated. Michelle lists Figma plus the implementation and OCR stack because part of the value is design engineering. Manolo lists Adobe XD, Sketch, Optimal Workshop, and WordPress in the context of being both lead UX designer and developer. Eemon’s strongest case, Dharmesh, and Niklas do not, in the report’s telling, derive credibility from a tools strip.

**Inferred (report):** Tools are evidence only when they explain capability or constraints. Otherwise they are metadata. The pitfall “tool résumé disguised as a case” says software proficiency is rarely the core hiring question.

## Implication for this portfolio

Do not put a tool cloud in Intro. Name a tool inside a story only where it explains how the work was possible or constrained.

## Limit

The report’s “rarely the core hiring question” is an inference. It did not survey hiring managers.
