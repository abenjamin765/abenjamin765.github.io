# The catalog browses by look first

**Claim:** Wall of Portfolios sorts by visual category before role. That is a property of the catalog, and a weak reason to pick this site’s theme.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`); category-count caveat in the winning-portfolios report.

## Evidence

**Observed (homepage notes):** Counts on the homepage: Minimalist 850+, Dark themed 400+, Interactive 320+, Creative 200+, Modern layout 90+. Browse order described: look, then role, years, country, company, and “open to work.” A directory card shows name, country, role, a style tag (Interactive, Creative, Dark Theme, Minimal), years of experience, and company logos. Profile pages are a thin frame: photo, name, role, country, years, company marks, then an iframe of the live site.

**Observed (E8):** Minimalist is the largest printed bucket. Confidence high for the directory UI.

**Inferred (homepage notes):** Recruiters using this catalog can filter for “minimal” or “dark” before they read a case. A distinctive first screen is how a portfolio gets sorted. It is not a substitute for the work list underneath it.

**Inferred (report):** Overlapping categories must not be used as a statistical denominator. See `../method-and-limits/visual-curation-bias.md`.

## Implication for this portfolio

Being legible in one glance matters. Choosing Minimalist because it is the largest bucket does not follow from the blurbs.

## Limit

The notes do not show that recruiters actually use the filter. “Can filter” is an inference from the UI.
