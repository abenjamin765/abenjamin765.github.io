# Images should answer a question in the story

**Claim:** The report says all 12 sites use visual media materially, and the useful image has a job: before and after, a state, an interaction, a prototype, a decision, or the final experience.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (report’s account of artifacts):** Joan Li’s ViewSonic case moves from research evidence through three explicit design iterations, a final navigation architecture, usability-testing results, and global rollout. Michelle Chen’s Pic2Split case includes a competitor baseline, trust research, flow comparison, wireframes, a version-one and version-two redesign, and measured completion times. Rishabh’s DayUp case uses survey and interview results, an illustrative delivery board, a decision log, multiple product states, principles, and a component system.

**Inferred:** “Every artifact should answer a question in the story.” A giant workshop-board screenshot that only proves a workshop occurred is called weak. A decision log that shows how a disputed priority was resolved is called strong. P1: product images explain before/after, states, iterations, or final behavior. P1 also says cut artifact theater.

## Implication for this portfolio

On a work story, add an image when it shows the decision or the change. Do not add a gallery as proof that the project existed. The homepage notes did not review images inside cases.

## Limit

“All 12 use visual media materially” is the report’s code. It does not say every image on those sites earns its place.
