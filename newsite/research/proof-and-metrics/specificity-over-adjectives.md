# Specific evidence beats adjective claims

**Claim:** The report says “strategic,” “user-centered,” and “data-driven” are weak without evidence, and that specificity is what convinced in the sample.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Inferred:** The comparison the report offers is those adjectives against concrete lines: “2.79% → 3.30% booking,” “171 survey responses + 12 interviews,” “+316% monthly search volume,” “61.7% → 82.4% onboarding conversion,” or a transparent statement that a product has not launched and therefore has no customer result to claim.

**Inferred pitfall:** “Generic seniority language.” “Strategic leader” is not evidence. The better move named is to show systems, decisions, adoption, and influence.

## Disagreement

The homepage notes agree that the claim is not a list of adjectives. They also say a precise sentence can be enough on the homepage until the case. Specificity is not only a number. Dharmesh’s “meals that are guaranteed to land within a set price” is the notes’ kind of specificity.

The “171 survey responses + 12 interviews” example is not tied to a named designer in the report’s summary. See `metric-types-in-the-sample.md`.

## Implication for this portfolio

The intro claim should be specific about the kind of work. Adjectives such as strategic or data-driven do not satisfy the notes or the report. A number is one way to be specific inside a case, not the only way on the row.

## Limit

The report does not count how often those three adjectives appear in the sample.
