# Quantification is common and not required on the row

**Claim:** The report coded numeric evidence in 10 of 12 portfolios. The homepage notes say a featured, praised portfolio (Dharmesh) does not lead with percentages. Both can be true, and they are not the same instruction.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`); Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (report):** Numeric evidence somewhere in prominent work or a reviewed case: 10/12. The report’s reading: quantification is common, but not universal, especially where NDA or confidentiality limits disclosure. The two sites without it are not named.

**Observed (homepage notes):** Dharmesh’s featured portfolio is praised for concise storytelling and does not lead with percentages. Vaibhav’s site puts numbers on the cards and in the positioning (“moves numbers”).

## Disagreement

The notes’ contradiction section says Vaibhav’s site argues that the hero should promise measurable impact, and Dharmesh’s and Niklas’s sites argue that a precise sentence is enough until the case. Both got onto the Wall. The report groups Vaibhav with outcome-first sites and still calls Dharmesh textbook-efficient because impact follows the scope decision inside the case. Leading the homepage with a number and having a number somewhere in a case are different bars.

## Implication for this portfolio

A work row may carry one outcome. The intro does not have to promise “moves numbers” for the page to match the featured set. Do not invent a percentage so the row resembles Vaibhav.

## Limit

10/12 is the report’s manual code of its sample. It is not a rate for the Wall, and it is not an audit of the numbers.
