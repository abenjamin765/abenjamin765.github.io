# Borrowed career guidance is not Wall evidence

**Claim:** Nielsen Norman Group, AIGA, and Smashing Magazine appear as citations inside the winning-portfolios report, not in the homepage notes. W3C is cited there at high level and, separately, by criterion number in the Metro × Apple report.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`); Metro × Apple design-system report (attachment `deep-research-report__11_.md`) for the WCAG 2.2 list.

## Evidence

**Borrowed (as the report summarizes it, not re-read here):**

- Nielsen Norman Group: curate portfolio evidence around strengths, specialization, process, and outcomes; hiring managers are time constrained and often scan; take the best case-study material into a presentation instead of scrolling the portfolio in an interview; rehearse; structured behavioral storytelling such as STAR; prepare examples of compromise, stakeholder work, and testing decisions.
- AIGA: no single mandatory portfolio format, project count, or case-study length. Earlier AIGA reporting, as the report describes it, said new UX hires commonly showed a handful of detailed projects.
- Smashing Magazine: the report says older portfolio research converges on the idea that a useful portfolio goes beyond screenshots and gives enough context to see how the designer works and why the work matters. The report does not quote Smashing’s findings in detail.
- W3C / WCAG, in the winning-portfolios report: keyboard operability, visible focus, and reflow under magnification rather than two-dimensional scrolling. That report points to these as implementation references, not as results from the 12-site audit.
- W3C / WCAG 2.2, in the Metro × Apple report: a longer AA mapping, including contrast ratios and target size. See `../design-system/wcag-22-aa-baseline.md`. Still not re-read from W3C, and still not a portfolio-sample result.

## Implication for this portfolio

When a file says “Borrowed,” it is secondhand guidance from the report. It is not an observation of the nine homepages, and it is not a hire rate.
