# This is not proof of what gets someone hired

**Claim:** Both sources can describe what a curated gallery shows. Neither can show what gets a designer hired.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`); winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (homepage notes):** The notes are a review of other portfolios, not interviews with hiring managers. The stated decision is what the first screen should say so a design director can tell, quickly, what kind of product designer this is. Confidence is moderate for “what featured homepages do” and low for “what Aaron’s site should do to be hired.”

**Observed (report):** Wall of Portfolios handpicks portfolios. The report’s own limits section says the analysis identifies features of work the Wall has already selected, not causal features proven to get someone hired.

**Inferred (report, stronger than its limits):** Later conclusions say “the level-appropriate story creates the hire” and that a winning portfolio is a designed argument about professional capability. Those sentences are the report’s synthesis. They are not a measured hiring outcome.

## Disagreement

The report’s closing lines are more confident than its limitations section, and more confident than the homepage notes. This library keeps the cautious claim: curated portfolios share an information pattern; that pattern is not evidence of hire rate.

## Implication for this portfolio

Treat the library as a constraint on clarity, curation, and honesty for the intro, experience, work, about, and contact panels. Do not treat it as a forecast that a layout will produce an offer.

## Limit

No hiring-manager interviews appear in either source. A pattern can be common on a portfolio gallery and still be the wrong emphasis for a specific loop.
