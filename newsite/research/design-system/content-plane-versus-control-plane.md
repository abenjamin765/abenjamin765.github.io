# Content plane and control plane

**Claim:** The report’s unified rule is a content plane and a control plane: material is how a platform renders a semantic surface, not the name of the token.

**Sources:** Metro × Apple design-system report (attachment `deep-research-report__11_.md`).

## Evidence

**Borrowed:** Apple, as the report summarizes current HIG, splits a content layer (application identity and distinctive content) from a UI layer (global navigation and actions, using familiar native components). The report calls this close to Metro’s “content, not chrome.” Apple’s guidance, as summarized, says glass generally does not belong in a flat content area when nothing meaningful is behind it to refract.

**Prescribed:** A token should say `surface-navigation`, not `liquid-glass`. On Apple that role may resolve to system glass; on Windows to Fluent material, stroke, or elevation; on the web to an opaque or translucent surface that still meets accessibility. Ordinary body sections do not get depth. A floating popover does.

**Inferred:** Classic Metro’s flatness and Apple’s control-layer glass look opposed. The report says Apple’s own content-layer limit is the reconciliation.

## Disagreement

The homepage notes put this site’s identity in the intro frame: yellow field, portrait, and the voice of the claim, with plain work rows (`../visual-identity/frame-versus-rows.md`). This report puts product identity in the content layer and treats the UI layer as native controls. Those instructions come from different sources. They are not averaged here.

## Implication for this portfolio

Do not wrap work rows in glass or cards for style. Whether the intro panel itself is “chrome” or “content” is not settled by this spec. The portfolio notes still describe that panel as the identity frame.
