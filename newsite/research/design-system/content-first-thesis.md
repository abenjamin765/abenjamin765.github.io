# Content first, native where it matters, semantic everywhere

**Claim:** The report’s closing thesis is content first, native platform behavior where it matters, and shared semantics everywhere. It says this is not a half-and-half visual mix.

**Sources:** Metro × Apple design-system report (attachment `deep-research-report__11_.md`).

## Evidence

**Prescribed:** The report’s suggested opening paragraph says the system inherits Metro’s typography, clarity, directness, purposeful motion, and content over chrome, and Apple’s platform familiarity, adaptive interfaces, system type, native controls, accessibility, and contextual materials. Use type, spacing, content, and hierarchy before decoration. Treat depth as evidence of a real layer. Use native UI for navigation and system interaction. Keep product identity primarily in content rather than by overriding familiar controls.

**Inferred:** The report says this is closer to both traditions than a pixel-level merger. That is the author’s conclusion about the cited docs, not a user test.

## Disagreement

The portfolio notes want a distinctive first screen (voice, color, portrait) and plain work rows. This thesis would put identity in content and keep controls familiar. See `content-plane-versus-control-plane.md` and `../visual-identity/frame-versus-rows.md`. Do not collapse that into one slogan for the intro.

## Implication for this portfolio

The sentence can inform later visual work: hierarchy before decoration, and no fake native material. It does not choose the claim in the intro, the work-row copy, or a hire story.

## Limit

The thesis is not evidence from hiring managers or from the Wall sample.
