# The closing argument is stronger than the evidence

**Claim:** The report ends with a level-by-level definition of what a portfolio “argues,” and then says the level-appropriate story creates the hire. The hire sentence is not supported by the study design.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Inferred (report):** A winning portfolio is a designed argument about professional capability. The report assigns these arguments: entry, I can learn, reason, iterate, and make evidence-based decisions; mid, I can independently get meaningful product work shipped; senior, I can make consequential decisions under ambiguity and connect them to outcomes; lead, I increase the quality and effectiveness of the people around me while staying grounded in product craft; staff, I create leverage across products, systems, and teams without needing direct authority; principal, I change how the organization frames, aligns around, and solves strategically important problems over time.

**Inferred (report):** “The visual site gets attention. The case evidence creates confidence. The level-appropriate story creates the hire.”

**Observed limit in the same report:** The sample cannot prove causal features that get someone hired. See `../method-and-limits/not-hiring-proof.md`.

## Implication for this portfolio

The staff sentence is the closest altitude to the question already asked in the homepage notes. It is still the report’s recommended argument, not an observed result for this site. The HTML title says “Software Designer,” which this paragraph does not define.

## Limit

Do not shorten the last sentence into a requirement. Attention, confidence, and hire are three different claims, and only the first is even partly about what these galleries show.
