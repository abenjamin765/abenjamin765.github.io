# Homepage order: identity, claim, work

**Claim:** On the homepages these sources describe, the person and one claim come first, and selected work is the next block.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`); winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (homepage notes, E1–E3):** Featured and sampled homepages introduce the person in one or two sentences: name, role, and where they work now. The next sentence is a single claim about the kind of work. Selected work is the next block, not a later page.

**Observed (report):** The dominant homepage structure is described as identity and role, then point of view, then selected work, then credibility, then human dimension, then contact. Examples: Dharmesh identifies himself as a senior product designer with six years of experience, gives a direct email, and moves into “a few things I've shipped.” Vaibhav reduces positioning to “A designer who moves numbers,” then four cases. Rishabh begins with “I grow design teams that move revenue.” Niklas positions himself at the intersection of product design, design engineering, and code.

**Inferred (report):** That is different from an about paragraph that could belong to any designer. The report calls the hero a hiring thesis.

## Disagreement

The report’s homepage chain includes credibility and a human dimension before contact. The homepage notes say about is a later, shorter block, and that experience, about, and a long biography should not sit in the intro. Both agree work should not wait for a biography. They do not agree on a required “human dimension” block.

## Implication for this portfolio

The intro panel can hold hello, name, role, and one claim. The work panel is the next argument. About stays a separate panel. Do not add a biography to the intro to satisfy the report’s “human dimension” phrase; the homepage notes explicitly keep that later.

## Limit

“Dominant structure” is the report’s reading. The homepage notes’ confidence for E1–E3 is high for their sample, not for all portfolios.
