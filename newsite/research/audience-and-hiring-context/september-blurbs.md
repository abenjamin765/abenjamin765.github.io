# September blurbs praise identity and clarity together

**Claim:** The September 2026 editorial blurbs, as recorded in the homepage notes, praise a distinctive visual identity and a short explanation of complex work. They do not praise length, tool lists, or motion for its own sake.

**Sources:** Wall of Portfolios notes (`newsite/research.md`); winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Observed (homepage notes, E9):** Blurbs name playful identity around Microsoft AI and platform work, concise storytelling of large consumer products, a clean visual system with clear project framing, business impact tied to decisions, and motion treated as part of the product rather than polish. Confidence is high for those five blurbs.

**Observed (report):** Eemon Roy is praised for AI experiences, systems thinking, playful identity, and craft. Dharmesh Nayak for concise storytelling and simplifying edge-case-heavy products. Aditya Muralidhar for clear framing, accessible and intentional interfaces, and consistent art direction. Vaibhav Verma for placing business impact, measurable outcomes, product thinking, and leadership at the center. The report says those descriptions corroborate patterns on the primary sites.

## Disagreement

The homepage notes include Rashmi’s motion feature in the set of five blurbs. The report treats her as outside the product-design analysis. “Motion as part of the product” is a homepage-notes observation about that blurb, not a finding that the twelve product sites feature motion.

The report’s Vaibhav blurb includes “leadership.” The homepage notes describe his feature as outcomes and numbers, and treat leadership-led homepages as a different pattern (Rishabh, and Shahi’s meta description).

## Implication for this portfolio

Identity on the intro panel and clarity on the work rows are the two curator compliments that match an individual-contributor product site. A leadership claim in the intro is a different homepage job. See `../what-not-to-copy/leadership-homepage-for-an-ic-site.md`.

## Limit

Blurbs are the Wall’s taste, not a hiring rubric. The report says they are useful because the Wall explains its own selection; that still is not an audit of why someone was hired.
