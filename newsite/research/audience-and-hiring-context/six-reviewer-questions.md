# Six questions a reviewer is trying to answer

**Claim:** The report says the strongest portfolios let a reviewer answer six questions unusually quickly.

**Sources:** Winning-portfolios report (attachment `deep-research-report__10_.md`).

## Evidence

**Inferred (report’s synthesis of its sample):** Who is this designer? What level do they operate at? What hard problem did they own? What did they personally decide? What changed because of the work? Would I trust them with the scope of the role I am hiring for?

**Borrowed:** The report says this aligns with Nielsen Norman Group guidance to curate around strengths, specialization, process, and outcomes, and with AIGA examples of a small set of substantial projects rather than an undifferentiated gallery. Those external texts were not re-read for this library.

## Implication for this portfolio

Intro can take the first two questions (who, and at what kind of altitude). Work rows can take the problem, the decision, and what changed. Trust in the scope of a role is not something this evidence can prove; it is the report’s name for the last question.

## Limit

The six questions are a reading of the sample, not a script from hiring managers. The homepage notes ask a narrower version: can a design director tell what kind of product designer this is.
