Amazon / Seller Central A-to-z First Claim

A first claim could teach.
The warning did not.

A seller learned that a customer had been refunded and an account metric was at stake, without a clear next action.

I championed coverage for eligible first claims and redesigned the notification around explanation, expectations, and prevention.

My contribution
UX Designer II; discovery synthesis, process mapping, tenets, coverage proposal, and email design
Partners
Seller Support, domain experts, leadership, legal, finance, and program partners
Evaluation
Reported program outcomes, 60 days after the U.S. launch

Support contacts revealed
what the warning left unclear.

When a customer’s order went wrong and the seller did not respond in time, Amazon could refund the customer and issue an A-to-z claim. The seller faced an Order Defect Rate impact and needed to understand what to do.

I reviewed 250 related Seller Support contacts. Appeals, disputes, and status questions accounted for a substantial share, and the analysis identified many as preventable. Seller interviews showed unclear actions and low confidence in preventing another claim.

I mapped messaging, eligibility, disputes, and appeals with stakeholders and domain experts. The issue was more than tone: sellers needed a useful explanation of the process and its consequences.

Invest in teaching
as well as resolving the claim.

I aligned partners on three tenets: make the first claim a learning opportunity, inform rather than discipline, and help sellers prevent another claim.

Investigating a first claim could cost as much as or more than the claim itself. I championed Amazon-funded coverage to resolve the customer issue quickly and teach the seller, bringing a proposal to leadership, legal, finance, and program partners.

Coverage applied to eligible first claims strictly under $50, with circumstance-specific limitations. I owned the proposal and email design; policy, funding, and delivery were shared program decisions.

Coverage created room for a learning moment. The email still had to explain the stakes and the boundaries accurately.

Explain the event.
Then make prevention actionable.

I structured the email around four jobs: claim details, an explanation of A-to-z and Order Defect Rate, eligible first-claim coverage, and practical prevention guidance. That sequence moved from the current event toward the seller’s next decision.

  1. Explain

    What happened, with a route to inspect the claim details.

  2. Set expectations

    What the claim means for Order Defect Rate and the account.

  3. State eligible coverage

    First claims strictly under $50, subject to circumstance-specific limitations.

  4. Coach

    Respond promptly, cancel out-of-stock orders, and keep product descriptions accurate.

What changed

A clearer path
after the first claim.

The project presentation reports program-level results 60 days after the U.S. launch: 88% of sellers who opened the updated email felt confident or very confident they could avoid future claims, and A-to-z-related Seller Support call volume fell 14%. These results cover the combined coverage and communication changes.

Additional reported program trend

The presentation also reported seller-initiated refunds trending toward a 30% year-over-year increase.

What I took forward

Specific explanations and prevention steps made the first-claim investment useful to the seller. I would separate notification effects from coverage effects in a future evaluation.
